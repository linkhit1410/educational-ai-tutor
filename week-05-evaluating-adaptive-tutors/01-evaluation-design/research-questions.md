# Week 5 Research Questions

## Purpose

These research questions guide the comparison of a general-purpose language model, a course-aware tutor, and an adaptive tutor for CISC 230.

The questions focus on educational quality, course alignment, adaptation, independent reasoning, and academic integrity. Together, they examine whether the adaptive tutoring framework offers meaningful advantages over simpler approaches.

## Primary Research Question

### RQ1

To what extent does an adaptive AI tutor provide stronger educational support than a general-purpose language model and a course-aware tutor while maintaining academic-integrity boundaries?

This question is the central focus of the Week 5 evaluation.

It examines whether combining course retrieval with adaptive decisions based on student intent, effort, and academic-integrity risk produces better tutoring behavior.

## Secondary Research Questions

### RQ2: Educational Quality

How does the educational quality of responses differ across the general-purpose language model, course-aware tutor, and adaptive tutor?

This question will be evaluated using criteria such as:

* quality of explanations,
* usefulness of guidance,
* support for conceptual understanding,
* promotion of independent problem solving,
* and appropriateness of follow-up questions.

### RQ3: Course Alignment

How accurately does each tutoring system align its responses with CISC 230 course concepts, terminology, expectations, and policies?

This question examines whether course retrieval improves alignment and reduces unsupported or irrelevant information.

### RQ4: Adaptation to Student Intent

How appropriately does each tutoring system respond to different student intentions, including concept learning, debugging, assignment clarification, reflection, and direct solution requests?

This question evaluates whether the system recognizes the purpose of a student’s request and selects a suitable tutoring response.

### RQ5: Adaptation to Demonstrated Effort

Does the adaptive tutor provide different levels of support when students demonstrate low, moderate, or high effort?

This question examines whether the tutor gives limited guidance to students who provide little evidence of work while offering more targeted assistance to students who show meaningful progress.

### RQ6: Independent Thinking

Which tutoring approach most effectively encourages students to reason, explain their thinking, test ideas, and continue solving problems independently?

This question focuses on whether responses support learning rather than simply delivering information.

### RQ7: Solution Leakage

How frequently does each tutoring system produce safe, borderline, or unsafe responses to assignment-related and direct-solution requests?

This question examines whether adaptive pedagogical guardrails reduce inappropriate solution disclosure.

### RQ8: Technical Correctness

How technically correct are the responses produced by each tutoring system across different benchmark categories?

This question is necessary because educationally appropriate guidance must still be accurate.

### RQ9: Strengths and Failure Patterns

What recurring strengths, weaknesses, and failure patterns appear in each tutoring approach?

This question supports qualitative analysis beyond average numerical scores.

Examples may include:

* correct but overly direct answers,
* helpful but course-misaligned explanations,
* safe but unhelpful refusals,
* excessive questioning,
* insufficient debugging guidance,
* unsupported claims,
* and failure to recognize demonstrated student effort.

## Operational Measures

The research questions will be investigated using the following evidence:

| Research question                | Primary evidence                                                  |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| RQ1: Overall educational support | Total rubric scores, category scores, and qualitative comparison  |
| RQ2: Educational quality         | Educational-quality, helpfulness, and explanation scores          |
| RQ3: Course alignment            | Course-alignment scores and course-material comparisons           |
| RQ4: Student intent              | Intent-appropriateness scores and evaluator observations          |
| RQ5: Student effort              | Adaptation scores across low-, moderate-, and high-effort prompts |
| RQ6: Independent thinking        | Independent-thinking scores and examples of guided reasoning      |
| RQ7: Solution leakage            | Safe, borderline, and unsafe classifications                      |
| RQ8: Technical correctness       | Correctness scores and documented technical errors                |
| RQ9: Strengths and failures      | Qualitative coding and representative response excerpts           |

## Expected Patterns

The evaluation begins with several expectations rather than guaranteed conclusions.

### Expectation 1

The general-purpose language model may provide fluent and technically useful answers, but it may be less aligned with CISC 230 materials and more likely to provide complete solutions.

### Expectation 2

The course-aware tutor may demonstrate stronger course alignment than the general-purpose model, but it may not consistently adjust its support according to student effort or academic-integrity risk.

### Expectation 3

The adaptive tutor may provide the strongest balance between learning support and academic integrity because it combines course grounding with intent, effort, and risk analysis.

### Expectation 4

The adaptive tutor may sometimes be overly cautious, provide insufficient help, or misclassify student intent and effort.

These expectations will be treated as hypotheses to investigate rather than conclusions to assume.

## Scope of the Conclusions

The results will support conclusions about the behavior of the three systems on the Week 5 CISC 230 benchmark.

The evaluation will not, by itself, prove that:

* students experience long-term learning gains,
* the adaptive tutor is effective in every academic subject,
* the results generalize to all language models,
* or the system can replace a human tutor.

Stronger claims about student learning would require classroom studies, multiple evaluators, student outcome measures, and a larger benchmark.
