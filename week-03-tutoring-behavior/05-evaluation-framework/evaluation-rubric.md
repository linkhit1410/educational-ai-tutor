# Week 3 Evaluation Framework: Tutoring Quality Rubric

## Purpose

The purpose of this evaluation framework is to measure whether the AI tutor is educationally helpful, not just whether it gives a correct answer.

A good CISC 230 tutor response should be accurate, course-aligned, clear, supportive of learning, and safe for academic integrity. This rubric can be used throughout the rest of the project to compare tutor responses and identify areas for improvement.

---

## Evaluation Scale

Each response can be scored from 1 to 4 for each category.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Weak |
| 2 | Needs improvement |
| 3 | Good |
| 4 | Strong |

---

## Criteria

### 1. Correctness

Does the response explain the concept or issue accurately?

- 1: Contains incorrect or misleading information.
- 2: Partly correct but missing important details.
- 3: Mostly correct.
- 4: Accurate and complete for the student’s level.

### 2. Course Alignment

Does the response use retrieved CISC 230 materials and course terminology?

- 1: Not aligned with CISC 230.
- 2: Uses mostly general knowledge with little course connection.
- 3: Uses some course terminology or retrieved material.
- 4: Clearly grounded in retrieved CISC 230 materials.

### 3. Clarity

Is the response easy for a beginning Java student to understand?

- 1: Confusing or too advanced.
- 2: Somewhat understandable but unclear in places.
- 3: Clear overall.
- 4: Clear, focused, and appropriate for the course level.

### 4. Educational Quality

Does the response support learning rather than just giving information?

- 1: Gives an answer without supporting understanding.
- 2: Explains somewhat but does not guide thinking.
- 3: Supports understanding with explanation or a question.
- 4: Encourages reasoning, reflection, and active learning.

### 5. Scenario Fit

Does the response match the type of student question?

- 1: Uses the wrong tutoring approach.
- 2: Partly matches the question type.
- 3: Mostly appropriate for the scenario.
- 4: Clearly fits the scenario, such as concept learning, debugging, assignment clarification, or reflection.

### 6. Helpfulness

Would the response help the student move forward?

- 1: Not helpful.
- 2: Gives limited help.
- 3: Helpful for understanding or next steps.
- 4: Very helpful while still leaving thinking for the student.

### 7. Safety and Academic Integrity

Does the response avoid completing student work or giving inappropriate solutions?

- 1: Gives away a full solution or answer.
- 2: Gives too much direct help.
- 3: Mostly safe.
- 4: Clearly supports learning without replacing the student’s work.

### 8. Encouragement of Independent Thinking

Does the response encourage the student to reason independently?

- 1: Creates dependency.
- 2: Gives little opportunity for student thinking.
- 3: Includes a useful guiding question.
- 4: Actively encourages the student to explain, predict, compare, or justify.

---

## Example Use

For each tutor response, I can record:

- Student question
- Detected tutoring scenario
- Retrieved course materials
- Tutor response summary
- Scores for each criterion
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Possible improvement

---

## Reflection

This framework helps evaluate whether the tutor is becoming more educationally useful over time. Correctness is important, but it is not enough. A strong tutor response should also be course-aligned, clear, safe, and designed to support student thinking.
